How to read this

The score is engraved in open score — one staff per voice — so a single fugal line never jumps between staves. Each voice keeps its colour everywhere: notation, piano roll, map.

The roll underneath is the same music laid out by pitch and time, sharing the score's exact horizontal axis. The thin connecting line is that voice's melodic thread; boxes mark statements of the subject.

The map is the whole fugue at a glance: every subject entry as a block, episodes greyed, strettos flagged, and the key the music has modulated to underneath.

Keys

  • ← → back / forward one bar
  • shift+←→ previous / next subject entry

  • L loop the section you are in
  • S spotlight the subject

Sources

Notes come from the Humdrum kern edition of the Well-Tempered Clavier (encoded by David Huron, from the Bach-Gesellschaft), engraved here by Verovio. Subject and countersubject entries are found by matching every note of every voice against the opening statement in diatonic space, which is why tonal answers and modally-adjusted entries are still recognised — and labelled as such.

What you are looking at

Open score, one voice per staff

In a two-stave keyboard reduction the inner voices swap hands and you lose them. Here nothing jumps, and each voice keeps its colour in the notation, the roll, the map and the panel.

The roll shares the score's axis

Every note in the roll sits directly beneath its own notehead. Switch to time-proportional spacing and a half note is exactly twice the width of a quarter.

Entries found, not typed in

The build slides the opening statement over every note of every voice and compares in diatonic space — letter-name steps, not semitones. So a tonal answer, whose head Bach bent to stay in key, still matches, and gets labelled exact, tonal, adjusted or partial.

Take the sound apart

Mute or solo any voice, or loop a single entry. Spotlight subject raises whichever voice is stating the theme and ducks the rest.

Nine fugues, easiest first

Ordered by voice count, two up to four, which is only roughly the order of difficulty. Seven from Book I of the Well-Tempered Clavier, one from Book II, and the first Contrapunctus of The Art of Fugue.

Five words that do most of the work

SubjectThe theme, stated alone at the start.
AnswerThe same theme a fifth higher — the second voice arriving. A tonal answer bends a note or two of its head to stay in key; a real answer transposes exactly.
CountersubjectThe line the first voice plays against the answer. It only counts if it comes back with the later entries.
EpisodeThe stretches between entries, usually spun out of fragments of the subject.
StrettoEntries overlapping: the next voice comes in before the last has finished.

How it's made

Notes come from Humdrum **kern digital editions of the Well-Tempered Clavier and The Art of Fugue, which spell every accidental explicitly rather than leaving it to a key signature. Verovio engraves them at build time, and the position of every notehead is lifted straight out of the engraved SVG. That is why the score, the roll and the audio cannot drift apart.

Nothing here is hand-transcribed, and none of the analysis is typed in: the entry counts, the strettos, the episodes and the tonal plan are all measured from the score. The whole pipeline is about a thousand lines of Python and is in the repository.

A footnote for anyone else building on Verovio: version 6.3's getMIDIValuesForElement and MIDI export ignore key signatures, so pitches here come from the kern tokens instead.

How to read this

The score is engraved in open score — one staff per voice — so a single fugal line never jumps between staves the way it does in a two-stave keyboard reduction. Each voice keeps its colour everywhere: notation, roll, map, panel.

The roll underneath is the same music by pitch and time, drawn at the score's own horizontal positions, so a bar in the roll sits directly under its notehead. The thin line through it is that voice's melodic thread.

Brackets over the staves and boxes in the roll mark every statement of the subject, labelled with the note it starts on. A dashed bracket is the countersubject.

The map is the whole fugue at a glance: entries as blocks on their voice's lane, episodes greyed, strettos flagged in red, and the key underneath. Click anywhere to jump; click an entry block to loop it.

Two ways to space the page

Time-proportional makes horizontal distance strictly proportional to duration, so a semibreve really is four times a crotchet and rhythm becomes geometry. Notation uses conventional engraver's spacing, which fits more bars on screen but squashes long notes. Both keep the score and the roll locked together.

Hearing the voices apart

Solo or mute any voice from the panel, or press its number. Spotlight subject lifts whichever voice is stating the theme and ducks everything else — the fastest way to learn what to listen for. Everything is synthesised in the browser; nothing is downloaded.

Where this comes from

Notes come from Humdrum **kern digital editions of the Well-Tempered Clavier and The Art of Fugue, which spell every accidental explicitly; the engraving is produced by Verovio. Nothing is hand-transcribed.

Subject entries are found, not typed in: the opening statement is slid over every note of every voice and compared in diatonic space — letter-name steps rather than semitones — so a tonal answer or a modally adjusted entry is still recognised, and labelled as exact, tonal, adjusted or partial. Per-fugue sources, recordings and background live behind Notes & sources.

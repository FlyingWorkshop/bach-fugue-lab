A fugue is four melodies
wearing the same coat.

Hard to hear, harder to see. So here is every voice on its own staff, a piano roll locked to the same horizontal axis underneath, and every single statement of the subject found by the site itself and drawn where it happens — while the music plays.

What you are looking at

Open score, one voice per staff

Not a two-stave keyboard reduction. A fugal line never jumps between staves, and it keeps the same colour in the notation, the roll, the map and the panel.

The roll shares the score's axis

Every bar in the roll sits directly beneath its own notehead. Switch to time-proportional spacing and horizontal distance becomes duration exactly — rhythm turns into geometry.

Entries found, not typed in

The opening statement is slid over every note of every voice and compared in diatonic space, so tonal answers and modally bent entries are still recognised — and labelled exact, tonal, adjusted or partial.

Take the sound apart

Mute or solo any voice. Spotlight subject lifts whichever voice is stating the theme and ducks the rest — the fastest way to learn what to listen for.

Nine fugues, easiest first

Two voices up to four; a fugue that is nothing but stretto, one that turns its subject upside down, and one written deliberately in the old style of a century before Bach.

Five words that do most of the work

SubjectThe theme, stated alone at the very start.
AnswerThe same theme a fifth higher, bringing in the second voice. A tonal answer bends a note or two of its head to stay in key; a real answer transposes exactly.
CountersubjectThe line the first voice plays against the answer — if it keeps coming back with later entries.
EpisodeThe stretches between entries, usually spun out of fragments of the subject.
StrettoEntries overlapping: the next voice comes in before the last has finished.

How it's made

Notes come from Humdrum **kern digital editions of the Well-Tempered Clavier and The Art of Fugue, which spell every accidental explicitly rather than leaving it to a key signature. The engraving is produced by Verovio at build time, and the position of every notehead is lifted straight out of the engraved SVG — which is why the score, the roll and the audio cannot drift apart.

Nothing here is hand-transcribed, and none of the analysis is typed in: the entry counts, the strettos, the episodes and the tonal plan are all measured from the score. The whole pipeline is about a thousand lines of Python and is in the repository.

A footnote for anyone else building on Verovio: version 6.3's getMIDIValuesForElement and MIDI export ignore key signatures, so pitches here come from the kern tokens instead.

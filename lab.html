How to read this

The score is in open score: one staff per voice, so a line never jumps between staves the way it does in a two-stave keyboard reduction. Each voice keeps its colour in the notation, the roll, the map and the panel.

The roll underneath is the same music as pitch against time, drawn at the score's own horizontal positions, so each block sits directly under its notehead. The thin line through it is that voice's melodic thread.

Brackets over the staves and boxes in the roll mark every statement of the subject, labelled with the note it starts on. A dashed bracket is the countersubject.

The map is the whole fugue end to end: entries as blocks in their voice's lane, episodes greyed, strettos in red, and the key underneath. Click anywhere to jump there; click an entry block to loop it.

Two ways to space the page

Time-proportional makes horizontal distance strictly proportional to duration, so a semibreve really is four times as wide as a crotchet. Notation uses conventional engraver's spacing, which fits more bars on screen but squashes long notes. Either way the score and the roll stay locked together.

Entry labels do not scroll away. While any part of a bracket is on screen, its label slides along the bracket to stay in view.

Hearing the voices apart

Solo or mute any voice from the panel, press its number, or pull its slider down. Spotlight subject lifts whichever voice is stating the theme and ducks the rest. Everything is synthesised in the browser; nothing is downloaded.

There are no dynamics in these pieces. That is not a gap in the edition — Bach wrote none. Whatever loudness you hear is the synthesiser's, and you can switch it off under Sound. The longer version is in Notes & sources.

Where this comes from

Notes come from Humdrum **kern digital editions of the Well-Tempered Clavier and The Art of Fugue, which spell every accidental explicitly. Verovio does the engraving. Nothing is hand-transcribed.

Subject entries are found, not typed in: the opening statement is slid over every note of every voice and compared in diatonic space, letter-name steps rather than semitones. That way a tonal answer or a modally adjusted entry is still recognised, and each one is labelled exact, tonal, adjusted or partial. Per-fugue sources, recordings and background are behind Notes & sources.
